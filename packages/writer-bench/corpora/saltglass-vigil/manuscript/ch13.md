# Chapter 13 — The Public Ledger

<!-- ref: ch13:p001 -->

King-tide morning began under a clear sky that made the lock visible from every upper terrace. Neris arrived at the Archive before first bell and opened the public evidence ledger. Not the red book. A new volume, white-bound, containing source copies, confidence ranges, superseded estimates, custody receipts, dissenting statements, and the current repair sequence.

The first page listed what the public needed to act: the supported cart mechanism, the narrowed trigger band, route order, repair window, and named stop authorities. Family facts about Hal's later message remained sealed. Pel's patient list remained private. Cael's uncharged accomplice remained part of the separate entry inquiry.

For every redaction, the public ledger named a category and custodian. PRIVATE MEDICAL—YARA. ACTIVE ENTRY INQUIRY—ISSA. FAMILY COMMUNICATION OUTSIDE LOCK CAUSE—NERIS AND YARA. A person could challenge the boundary without seeing the protected content. Neris had learned that a blank space invited either trust or conspiracy. A labeled boundary invited review.

Truthful disclosure did not require making every secret public. It required showing why each withheld fact was outside the decision.

<!-- ref: ch13:p002 -->

Orra entered without her director's coat. She wore service gray and carried her keys in an evidence pouch.

“Oversight convenes after the tide,” she said. “I have transferred classification authority to Issa and Sera for the operation.”

Neris checked the written transfer. It preserved Orra's ability to advise and removed her ability to exclude a reading from the trigger series. Two lower-ward observers had signed.

“Your Archive key?” Neris asked.

“Remains mine until oversight decides employment. My supplemental-series key is surrendered.”

She placed it on the public desk. The act did not absolve eleven years. It made one future concealment harder.

Orra also supplied the index of every supplemental classification still in the Archive. Four concerned harmless instrument noise, one an unresolved kiln reading, and two the west bearing. Neris did not have time to review them before threshold, so Issa sealed the index for oversight. Disclosure could be incomplete without becoming another private promise, provided the boundary and next owner were visible.

Neris signed receipt. Neither called it forgiveness.

<!-- ref: ch13:p003 -->

At the market court, Issa read the current ledger aloud. The comptroller named their procurement approval before naming the clerk's compression. Sera named the rule that engineers had failed to place on carts. Orra named both deviations and the kiln reset. Neris named her apprentice alteration and the difference in stakes.

The city heard a chain instead of a scapegoat.

Neris used Hal's carried-harmonic map only for the mechanism it helped test. She did not display his inland route or Yara's destroyed letter. She showed the controlled rawhide and iron traces side by side, then the wheel scars, seam grains, gear wear, missed noise reports, and classification history. Each link cited a record. Removing one weakened the claim but did not make another link disappear.

Some demanded one anyway. A merchant blamed the Low Choir for delay. A glassworker blamed council for every iron pass. A carter asked whether her wages would be seized for following permits. Issa stated that emergency repair would not decide civil liability.

“Today we stop the load,” Neris said. “Afterward, evidence assigns each action without inventing intent.”

<!-- ref: ch13:p004 -->

The first pressure reading at 03:42 remained below trigger. Sera signed it into the public ledger. The quiet chorus began route readiness, though it had never fully stopped. Shutter captains checked tracks. Clinic teams staged stretchers. District keepers tested clappers once.

Neris carried the sealed master key to the Bellhouse. Tovan walked beside her but did not touch the pouch.

At the carriage room, Janek verified the key receipt. Pel set the code bar without tensioning the ropes. Issa held the council plate in reserve. Janek held the bellkeeper plate.

“Outer release at verified threshold only,” Neris read.

“And public notice aligned,” Tovan added.

The footnote, requested and present.

They inspected the pouch seal together. Tovan confirmed it had not been opened since transfer. Neris confirmed his right to request return remained active until threshold admission. He declined without ceremony and signed the morning custody line. The exchange repeated yesterday's trust under new pressure; neither treated one agreement as permanent permission. Janek placed the pouch in the carriage safe where two witnesses, not Neris alone, would open it after her verified call.

<!-- ref: ch13:p005 -->

Cael stood outside the operator square with their observation sheet. They had signed no readiness role, but Neris had granted witness access under the same limits as council and Low Choir observers.

“Pressure?” they asked.

“Below early bound by three units.”

“Rate?”

“Higher than slow projection, inside current range.”

Cael wrote both. Tovan watched their pencil rather than their hands.

“If threshold crosses,” Neris said, “I verify and release. Janek or Issa releases second. Pel sounds only after shutter receipt.”

“I know the catechism.”

“State your delay condition.”

Cael read from chapter twelve's sheet: “Posted threshold, no carriage release after one full district receipt cycle.”

No private definition waiting to expand under fear.

<!-- ref: ch13:p006 -->

Neris went to the seaward lock before the next reading. The repair crew had staged every tool in order. Replacement craze plates rested in padded cases. The bronze sleeve gauge cleared the lower flood door. Rawhide-rimmed carts waited above the boundary.

Sera assigned positions. Two mechanics at the west pivot. One at the float. Neris on measurement and public receipts. Sera on physical abort. Orra outside the gallery as consulting engineer, with no command authority.

The arrangement hurt Orra. She accepted it.

“If the second bearing lags?” Neris asked.

“We stop unload, inspect alignment, and reload if the lag exceeds twelve seconds.”

“If the tide reaches blue first?”

“Reload and exit lower. No plate is worth trapping a crew.”

Every crew member repeated the abort rule and named the person whose call they would follow. One mechanic initially said Orra from years of habit. He corrected to Sera and marked his helmet strap blue as a visual reminder. The tide clerk carried two timers, one mechanical and one measured against Bellhouse strikes. The lower flood wheel had a dedicated operator who performed no repair task. Specialization looked wasteful until the door became the only exit.

<!-- ref: ch13:p007 -->

At 04:03, the west plate brightened. The branch remained below trigger but grew across two ruled divisions in one load cycle. Neris called the figures through the speaking tube. Pel repeated them. The public clerk posted them. The chorus carried the same words.

No bell.

The city continued readiness. Shutters stayed open enough for trade but all cross aisles had guides. Upper gates remained closed. A ferry completed its final inbound crossing and held outside the inner chain as planned.

Neris watched the curve rather than the expected minute. The recovered range shaped attention; it did not command the glass to obey.

<!-- ref: ch13:p008 -->

At 04:11, the line accelerated. Sera checked wind, tide, and frame orientation. Maker's notch seaward. No mirrored error. The automatic strip aligned to recovered Bellhouse time.

“One unit below early trigger,” Neris called.

Orra stood behind the gallery line with both hands empty. “The old deviations rose in two steps,” she said. “Then held.”

Useful history, offered without authority over its classification.

Sera asked, “Does that change the trigger?”

“No,” Neris answered. “Current plan uses current wear.”

Orra accepted the limit and added a detail without pressing its conclusion: the second old deviation had occurred after a Stores delivery delayed at the west bay. The route petition in Hal's tube independently named the same delay. Neris recorded the corroboration and kept the current threshold unchanged. Old evidence could strengthen the causal history without gaining authority over a present control already tested.

They waited through the next gate load. Holding to a declared threshold felt different from waiting for certainty. One protected coordination. The other would have protected indecision.

<!-- ref: ch13:p009 -->

The line crossed at 04:18:51, inside the recovered range.

Neris read the plate twice. Sera independently read. Both signed.

“Threshold met,” Neris said into the tube. “West branch eleven point four. Time 04:18:51. Initiate posted sequence.”

Pel repeated every figure. The public clerk wrote them correctly. Tovan's chorus echoed the notice through district chains.

At the Bellhouse, Neris's key waited in Janek's custody under sealed receipt. The outer plate release required her verification but not her physical return. The plan authorized Janek to open the pouch after her signed threshold arrived through two channels.

Custody moved by rule, not ownership.

In the market court, the public notice turned from blank form to event record. People stopped asking whether threshold might occur and began asking which step was underway. The transition produced noise, but not an undirected rush. Clinic volunteers raised blue flags. Ferry captains held their chains. Upper-gate guards displayed the corrected cards outward so the crowd could see they were waiting on lower receipts.

<!-- ref: ch13:p010 -->

Three east bells did not sound yet. First, market shutter receipt.

Through the tube came clipped reports. North aisle closing. Clinic lane clear. Ferry carts held. One spice stall resisted until the owner saw the posted threshold rather than hearing a rumor about it. Shutter captains sent checks.

Neris used the interval to begin controlled unload preparation. Mechanics tensioned the reload line. Sera marked the abort gauge. The tide remained below blue with seventy-three projected minutes.

“One less than rehearsal,” a mechanic said.

“Wind shifted south,” Sera replied. “We do not recover it by moving faster.”

They recovered it by removing no safety step.

<!-- ref: ch13:p011 -->

The speaking tube announced outer plate released, bellkeeper plate released, bar verified. Pel ready west. Janek observing.

Then Pel's voice changed. “Cael witness absent from station.”

Tovan answered from the market chain. “Searching tower route. Continue verification.”

Neris did not halt. Cael's absence from a witness role was not a failed dependency. Unauthorized carriage entry would be. Pel and Janek still controlled the room.

“Shutter receipts complete,” the tube said.

The first three bells struck east.

The sound entered the lock through stone and water. Neris did not touch a rope or order a strike. She had promised she would not ring the bell. The authorized sequence was larger than the promise Orra had once inferred.

<!-- ref: ch13:p012 -->

During the six-count pause, blue-walk guides began lower movement. The crew unloaded the west pivot one controlled turn. Load transferred to the second bearing.

Its response lagged.

Not twelve seconds. Seven. Within the abort limit and longer than rehearsal.

Sera held one hand raised. “No second turn until it seats.”

The gallery clock counted. At nine seconds the second bearing settled with a blunt knock.

“Continue,” Sera said.

Three west bells sounded above them, Pel's rhythm slightly quicker than Janek's but the pause exact. District receipts began.

The lock had entered the repair sequence. Every later action would occur under rising water.

<!-- ref: ch13:p013 -->

The first craze plate came free at minute twelve. Behind it, wear had spread farther along the gear sleeve than the external trace predicted. Sera inserted the bronze gauge. It stopped two marks short.

“Sleeve first,” she ordered.

The staged repair already reflected that sequence. Tools arrived without crossing hands. Neris recorded each removal and condition while watching the pressure strip. The west branch fell as load came off, supporting the mechanism in real time.

Sera photographed the decline before allowing the old plate into its case. The mechanic who had defended the permanent lens called out notch orientation without being prompted. Another checked that the removed plate's historic craze was marked so future auditors would not mistake it for damage incurred during repair. The crew had absorbed the inquiry's distinctions into work: old versus new, observed versus inferred, replaced versus erased.

Outside, district receipts completed. Upper gates opened only after lower flow was established. No counterflow appeared on the route board.

The plan held. The bearing had not promised to make that sufficient.

<!-- ref: ch13:p014 -->

At minute twenty-seven, a reload-line knot began to slip. A mechanic caught it before loss and called halt. Sera froze work. The crew replaced the knot with a rated splice from the contingency roll.

Orra saw the replacement would add four minutes. “The old line can hold through sleeve seating,” she said from behind the boundary.

Sera asked Neris to record the advice, then rejected it. “Physical halt remains mine. New splice.”

Orra stepped back. No appeal to rank.

The new line tested clean at minute thirty-three. Work resumed. Authority had cost six minutes including test. It had also prevented an unmeasured line from becoming the crew's hidden margin.

<!-- ref: ch13:p015 -->

The first replacement plate seated at minute forty-one. The second bearing lagged again when they shifted the load, this time ten seconds. Still below abort. Closer.

Neris's body asked for a single conclusion. Safe or failing. The gauges provided conditions instead: lag ten, abort twelve, tide below blue, reload line verified, sleeve halfway seated.

“State the range,” Sera said.

“Two seconds to abort limit. Twenty-nine projected minutes below blue. Sleeve needs twelve to sixteen.”

“Decision?”

Neris felt the old reflex to return the question to certainty. “Continue sleeve. No second plate until lag rechecked.”

Sera nodded. Shared choice, named conditions.

The decision exposed Neris whichever way the next reading moved. If lag reached twelve, continuing the sleeve would be examined. If they reloaded too early, the unresolved branch would remain. She entered her reasoning before the outcome could edit her memory: sleeve seating reduced known wear; tide margin supported one test; second plate remained conditional. Accountability meant leaving a record that could later show she had chosen badly.

<!-- ref: ch13:p016 -->

At minute fifty, Issa's voice came through the tube. All public routes stable. No injuries. Low Choir observers requested the complete repair status.

Neris dictated it: one plate replaced, sleeve in progress, second-bearing lag ten seconds against twelve-second abort, twenty minutes plus uncertainty to blue mark. She did not wait for success to make the operation legible.

The chorus repeated the risk exactly. A market captain asked whether ten seconds meant the bearing had failed. The district reply said no: it meant continue under a twelve-second halt. Another listener asked why continue so close. The answer named remaining tide time and the unsafe branch under partial reload. Public explanation consumed attention but not gallery time because the channels had been prepared before threshold.

Orra listened. “Publishing the lag may draw people back toward the lock.”

“Routes control access. Concealment does not.”

Orra looked toward the lower flood door, then signed the status as witness. Her arc from control to accountability did not need the word arc. It existed in the signature attached to risk she could no longer shape alone.

<!-- ref: ch13:p017 -->

The sleeve seated at minute fifty-seven. Sera ordered a partial reload to test alignment before the second plate. The bearing took weight, hesitated, and settled at eleven seconds.

One below abort.

“Hold,” Sera said.

The tide clerk called twelve minutes to blue.

Through the speaking tube, Pel reported movement at the Bellhouse roof stair. Tovan's voice followed: “Cael heading for carriage level. No key in their possession.”

Neris could not leave the gallery. She could not direct Tovan's sibling. She could verify what mattered to her domain.

“Carriage state?”

“Released, code complete, ropes secured. Janek and Pel present.”

“Then no operational halt. Report unauthorized contact.”

<!-- ref: ch13:p018 -->

Sera faced the final choice. Replace the second craze plate and risk missing blue, or reload with the old plate after repairing the sleeve. The plan allowed partial completion if the branch fell below tolerance.

Neris read the glass. The fork had narrowed but remained above safe return load. “Plate required.”

“Agreed,” Sera said. “Eight-minute install. Reload at six if it resists.”

They opened the second case.

Above, the Bellhouse had finished its warning, but Cael was climbing toward a carriage whose outer plate remained released. Below, the tide rose. The second bearing held eleven seconds from abort.

Neris set the replacement plate into Sera's hands and began the final count.

She counted conditions, not hopes: eight minutes planned, six to forced reload, eleven-second lag, twelve-second stop, tide mark rising. The same city that had once asked her for one perfect minute now waited on a range made public. Whatever happened next would not be clean. It would be attributable.
