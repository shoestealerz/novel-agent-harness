# Chapter 3: Below the Customs House

<!-- ref: ch03:p001 -->
The customs tunnel opened behind a rack of obsolete flags. Iven had shown Mara the entrance when they were twelve and sworn it led beneath the sea. In truth it sloped only to the old tide cells, where smugglers once waited beside their confiscated cargo. The winter flood had filled the lower chambers with silt. Recent boot prints crossed that silt now, sharp at the heel and overlaid by the circular marks of an oil-jar crate.

<!-- ref: ch03:p002 -->
Mara entered alone during the ledger clerk’s supper hour. She carried Orin’s brass compass, a shuttered lamp, and the silver key from the observatory safe. The key opened the third tide cell. Beyond it, shelves held hundreds of empty oil jars, each washed clean and dated. The newest bore tomorrow’s date. A copper pipe climbed from the back wall toward the northern cliff, too narrow for passage but wide enough to carry oil—or sound.

<!-- ref: ch03:p003 -->
On a desk she found Councilor Hadrin’s maintenance schedule. Oil traveled through the pipe to a reservoir beneath the lighthouse. The reservoir fed not the lantern but a mechanism labeled the echo chamber. Every new moon, the council sounded the harbor bell once and used the chamber to answer from the cliff. The paired tones, Orin’s notes suggested, kept the glass tide confined to the northern inlet. If the interval failed, retained reflections could drift into town.

<!-- ref: ch03:p004 -->
Mara read the explanation twice. It was either careful engineering or a superstition with excellent handwriting. A second page listed eight years of successful intervals. The entry for the coming night was blank. Beside it Hadrin had written: LENS REQUIRED. SUBSTITUTE UNSTABLE. The stolen lighthouse lens was not merely property. It focused the bell’s vibration through the reservoir, although none of Orin’s diagrams explained why light should govern sound.

<!-- ref: ch03:p005 -->
Footsteps scraped beyond the shelves. Mara closed her lamp shutter. Iven entered carrying a full oil jar. In darkness, his breathing sounded like someone turning pages too quickly. He set the jar beneath the pipe and said, “You should have asked me to bring you.” Mara opened the shutter. The accusation she had prepared collapsed when she saw blood soaking his blue wrist cord. He had cut his palm on the cliff gate and carried the oil here after finding the outer path watched.

<!-- ref: ch03:p006 -->
Iven admitted his betrayal without being asked. Three months ago, Orin had sent him the final sketch and instructions to give both to Mara if the echo chamber began to fail. Iven instead gave the sketch to Hadrin, believing the council could repair the mechanism without dragging Mara back into her father’s obsession. The clean rectangle on Orin’s table had been the framed original. Hadrin found no lens behind it, and Iven had delayed writing to Mara until the second bell began sounding at the wrong hours.

<!-- ref: ch03:p007 -->
“Where is my father?” Mara asked. Iven said he did not know. The ledger entries bearing O.V. arrived inside sealed jars sent through the cliff pipe. He had never seen who wrote them. He had preserved the possibility of Orin’s survival because the alternative made his choices unforgivable. Mara understood this and did not forgive him. Some understandings narrowed mercy instead of enlarging it.

<!-- ref: ch03:p008 -->
The pipe rang beneath Iven’s hand. Once. Twice. The second vibration came almost immediately, without the measured pause described in Hadrin’s schedule. All along the shelves, drops of wash water lifted from the empty jars and held their shapes in the air. In each suspended drop Mara saw the tide cell as it had been moments before: herself entering alone, lamp raised, silver key bright between her fingers.
