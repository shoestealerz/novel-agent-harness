# Chapter 1: The Dead Light

<!-- ref: ch01:p001 -->
The ferry found Aster Reach by following bells through the fog. Mara Vale stood at the forward rail while the harbor assembled itself in pieces: a black roof, three wet pilings, the pale square of the customs house. Above them all, the lighthouse waited on the northern cliff with its lantern room blind. Eight years had passed since the winter flood, but the tower still looked freshly extinguished, as if someone had pinched the flame only moments before her arrival.

<!-- ref: ch01:p002 -->
She had seen the lighthouse throughout her childhood without ever seeing it in person. Her father drew it on wrapping paper, on invoices, in the margins of letters he never sent. Orin Vale rendered every stone precisely and then covered the lantern room with charcoal until the paper shone. When Mara once asked what stood behind the black, he told her that a closed room could be a kindness. He disappeared three months later, leaving his final sketch under her breakfast plate.

<!-- ref: ch01:p003 -->
Iven Saye waited beneath the customs awning, taller than memory and thinner than his coat. He did not embrace her. He took the smaller of her two cases and said, “The harbor ledger closes at six.” It was the sort of sentence he used when another sentence frightened him. As children, they had crossed roofs together and lied with matching confidence. Now he wore the blue cord of a harbor clerk around his wrist and looked toward the cliff only when he thought she was watching the rain.

<!-- ref: ch01:p004 -->
“Has the light ever come back?” Mara asked as they climbed Salt Street. Iven shook his head. The lighthouse had been dark since the flood, he said, and no appointed keeper had entered it since. The council kept the cliff gate locked. Fishermen still left oil at the lower steps on the first night of every new moon, but that was habit, not belief. When Mara asked who collected the oil, Iven adjusted his grip on her case and began describing the repairs to the western pier.

<!-- ref: ch01:p005 -->
The sea below them changed color with every turn of the road. It was iron beside the breakwater, green beneath the gulls, and for one breath the bruised violet of her mother’s old shawl. Mara distrusted anyone who claimed the sea was blue. Blue was a color for maps and promises, things that became simpler when made flat. The actual water took whatever color the watcher feared most and kept none of them long enough to be accused of lying.

<!-- ref: ch01:p006 -->
Orin’s house had acquired a red council seal across the front door. Iven produced the release form before Mara could ask, already signed in three places. “As executor, I was permitted to air the rooms,” he said. The word executor struck harder than any account of death. Orin had never been declared dead. His boat was found tied inside the flooded customs tunnel, his boots beneath its bench, and no body anywhere along the coast.

<!-- ref: ch01:p007 -->
Inside, dust softened the furniture but left a clean rectangle on the worktable. Something large had recently been removed. Mara set her palm against the bare wood. Iven said the council had taken no property. He said it too quickly, then went to light the stove. In the drawer below the rectangle, Mara found a sliver of charcoal, a brass compass that pointed stubbornly toward the cliff, and a note in her father’s hand: WHEN THE HARBOR RINGS TWICE, DO NOT FOLLOW THE SECOND BELL.

<!-- ref: ch01:p008 -->
The first bell sounded before sunset. It rolled through the walls and trembled in the empty cups. Iven stopped with the match still burning between his fingers. A second tone followed from farther away—not from the harbor tower, Mara thought, but from the dead lantern room on the cliff. Iven crushed the match against the stove. “There was only one,” he said. Mara folded the note into her sleeve and understood that whatever else had changed in Aster Reach, they no longer lied with matching confidence.
