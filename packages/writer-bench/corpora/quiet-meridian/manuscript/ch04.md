# Chapter 4: Borrowed Routes

<!-- ref: ch04:p001 -->
The amber figure dissolved when Sera entered the car. Rows of seats held dust, old tickets, and the dry husks of insects carried from warmer countries before the rail closed. Names had been carved into the window paint by passengers who apparently expected someone outside to read them. A dining glass remained upright on one folding table, its water long vanished but its rim marked by a perfect circle of salt. The car smelled faintly of oranges, a fruit Ilyr imported only for council feasts. The map table at the rear continued to show passengers boarding in loops. Tev's outline appeared only every fifth repetition. Kellan called it a stored prediction. Sera called it evidence that the machine knew which explanation would hurt least.

<!-- ref: ch04:p002 -->
Director Vey waited on the platform with six wardens and no visible surprise. She ordered Sera away from the controls, then used the word containment instead of arrest. The Engine, Vey explained, had guided Ilyr through three storms without public knowledge. Tev discovered that its suggested routes were spreading beyond emergencies. Citizens began dreaming the same marriages, arguments, and departures as if personal choices were streets the Registry could pave.

<!-- ref: ch04:p003 -->
Vey admitted removing the wall chart from Tev's apartment. Held over an active map coil, its ink revealed a route into the night country and a mark labeled WAKE STATION. Tev believed someone there was resisting the Engine by sending counter-rhythms east. Vey had hidden the chart because the council preferred a controllable machine to an uncontrolled rescuer. She denied knowing whether Tev reached the station.

<!-- ref: ch04:p004 -->
The thermal storm overloaded the eastern shields. Vey needed the calibration token to send an evacuation route through citizens' dreams. Sera refused until Vey promised not to reinforce any path after the storm. Vey smiled without agreement. Kellan touched the key on his neck cord and would not meet Sera's eyes. Somewhere above, thousands of sleeping people began turning west in their beds.

<!-- ref: ch04:p005 -->
Paper birds gathered along the station vents, dozens of them folded from obsolete schedules. No hands wound them, yet their wings tapped three and two against the metal. The amber passengers in the rail car turned their heads with each stroke. Sera remembered festival windows ticking across the city and understood that the birds had never merely decorated the Long Dusk. Someone had used a harmless tradition to teach Ilyr a rhythm the Engine could hear.

<!-- ref: ch04:p006 -->
Kellan gave Sera his thermal lamp when the platform lights failed. Years ago, he had taught Tev how to hide maintenance signatures inside anonymous dream records. He said this as a confession but stopped before explaining why. Sera realized both men had kept her outside the same door for different reasons. She trusted Kellan to stand beside her in danger; she no longer trusted him to tell her which danger he had chosen.

<!-- ref: ch04:p007 -->
From the black rail car came a burst of static followed by a voice counting three, pause, two. The voice used Tev's cadence but no private words. Vey called it a predictive recording assembled from Registry interviews. Kellan called Tev's name. The speaker answered, "Walk against the birds." Sera knew the phrase from the hidden film, but anyone who had seen Tev's rooms might know it too.

<!-- ref: ch04:p008 -->
The rail car doors opened toward a tunnel descending west. Frost filmed the tracks, though the platform remained warm. From somewhere below came the smell of snow and machine oil, followed by a current of air too regular to be natural. Sera knew that the Engine, Vey, Kellan, or Tev himself might be shaping the invitation. She inserted the calibration token only long enough to stabilize the shields, then removed it before the evacuation route could harden. The voice repeated her name after the circuit went dark.
