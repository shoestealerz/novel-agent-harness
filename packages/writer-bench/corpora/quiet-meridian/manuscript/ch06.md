# Chapter 6: The Night Country

<!-- ref: ch06:p001 -->
Beyond the heat pipes, the tunnel entered the night country. Frost thickened into translucent roots across the walls. Sera and Kellan wore emergency masks while the portable coil warmed a circle of air around them. Sound altered in the cold: their breath arrived late, and the scrape of a boot seemed to continue several steps after the foot lifted. Pale mineral reeds grew between the sleepers, chiming when heat touched them. No city maintenance mark appeared here, yet the rail ties had been cleared of ice by recent hands. The black rail car had stopped beside an underground lake whose surface reflected stars no window could see. Tev's wall chart showed the same lake as an empty square.

<!-- ref: ch06:p002 -->
As children, Sera, Tev, and Kellan had played route games in the unbuilt foundations beneath their district. Tev always drew the destination first. Kellan invented rules for reaching it. Sera found the route neither of them expected. Remembering this, she saw the Engine's weakness: it could amplify paths people imagined, but perhaps it could not originate a choice made without a declared destination.

<!-- ref: ch06:p003 -->
The sky above the lake existed only in reflection, yet it changed as she watched: black near Kellan's boots, silver beneath the rail car, then the burnt orange of Ilyr's eastern shields. Sera did not call any color false. The meridian had taught her that light described a relationship, not an object. The lake was borrowing possible skies, and each possibility revealed the fear of the person standing over it.

<!-- ref: ch06:p004 -->
Paper birds floated across the lake with their wings open. Their clock springs had been removed, so they kept no rhythm. Sera stepped onto the first and found it supported her weight like thin ice. Kellan protested that the route was impossible. "Then don't teach it what possible means," she said. They crossed by placing each unwound bird where no map predicted the next step.

<!-- ref: ch06:p005 -->
At the far shore, the Wake Station occupied a cavern of dark map glass. Hundreds of citizens slept in narrow rooms, each wearing a coil that fed counter-rhythms east. Their names matched people officially lost to storms over three decades. Some had aged normally. Others appeared no older than their disappearance photographs. The apparatus sustained their bodies while distributing their routes through Ilyr's dreams.

<!-- ref: ch06:p006 -->
Tev's name appeared on the station roster, assigned to Chamber Nine. The entry began the night he vanished and showed continuous neural activity, but the identity field carried a warning: COMPOSITE ROUTE—SOURCE COUNT UNKNOWN. His empty suit and maintenance signatures now had a physical explanation, though not a simple one. A living body might occupy the chamber, or the station might have assembled Tev from every memory of him sent through the Registry.

<!-- ref: ch06:p007 -->
Kellan asked permission before opening the next door. Sera noticed the change and gave it without softening. Trust had not returned; a better habit had begun. He admitted that Tev once proposed using Sera's unusual sleep maps to separate chosen routes from imposed ones. Kellan refused because the experiment could trap her in the network. His refusal had been the last decision about her he made without asking.

<!-- ref: ch06:p008 -->
Chamber Nine stood open. The bed was warm and empty, its coil still tracing a three-and-two pulse. On the pillow lay Tev's childhood compass and a new strip of map film. The film showed Sera and Kellan crossing the paper birds moments earlier, followed by a final line not yet drawn. Condensation formed on the blank section as though an invisible fingertip were considering where to begin. From deeper in the station came footsteps matching Sera's pace exactly, stopping whenever she stopped.
