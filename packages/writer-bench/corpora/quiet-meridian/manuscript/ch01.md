# Chapter 1: The Line Without Noon

<!-- ref: ch01:p001 -->
The city of Ilyr had no noon. It lay along the quiet meridian, the narrow country between the white furnace of the dayside and the blue-black ice of night. At the eastern windows, sunlight rested like a blade that never rose or fell. At the western gates, frost smoked from every hinge. Between those extremes, neighborhoods measured wealth by temperature: glass towers nearest the light, crowded stone in the temperate band, blue-painted dormitories where pipes froze every artificial night. Public clocks displayed work shifts instead of hours because the sky offered no change to count. Sera Nahl rode the boundary tram between them, carrying a case of obsolete maps and the notice that her missing brother's rooms would be opened at last.

<!-- ref: ch01:p002 -->
Sera made sleep maps for the Registry: diagrams assembled from the routes citizens remembered walking in dreams. Most were harmless repetitions of markets, staircases, or childhood kitchens. A few contained streets that had never been built. Her brother Tev had called those impossible routes the city's second skeleton. He vanished eighteen months ago while surveying an abandoned rail beneath the meridian, leaving behind an empty thermal suit and a recorder full of wind.

<!-- ref: ch01:p003 -->
Kellan Rusk waited outside Tev's building with the registry key already in his hand. He and Sera had once translated each other's silences without effort. Since Tev vanished, every kindness between them had required paperwork. Kellan offered condolences as though submitting a form, then admitted he had delayed the opening order three times. "Dust preserves more than clerks do," he said. Sera heard the apology beneath the sentence and chose not to accept it.

<!-- ref: ch01:p004 -->
On the sill above Tev's door sat a paper bird folded from a transit schedule. A clock spring hidden in its breast made it tap one wing against the glass: three quick strokes, a pause, then two. Children throughout Ilyr built such birds during the Long Dusk festival, winding them until every window ticked. Tev used to say the city borrowed birds because the real ones had followed the sun west and never learned to return.

<!-- ref: ch01:p005 -->
The permanent sky changed color as Sera turned the bird in her hands. Near the hot horizon it was copper, overhead the gray of erased pencil, and above the ice a green so thin it seemed remembered rather than seen. Tourists called Ilyr's sky violet because that was the color printed on railway posters. Sera distrusted any color that stayed the same in a sentence. Light on the meridian was an argument between where one stood and what one feared.

<!-- ref: ch01:p006 -->
A black Registry seal crossed Tev's apartment door. Kellan broke it and recorded the time. Inside, the rooms smelled of mineral dust and the bitter resin used to clean map glass. Someone had removed the wall chart above Tev's desk; four bright nail heads remained around a rectangle of unfaded plaster. Kellan said the Registry had inventoried nothing. He said it slowly, with the careful precision he used when a broader truth would indict him.

<!-- ref: ch01:p007 -->
The official report called Tev dead by exposure. Sera had read it until the words became shapes. His thermal suit had been found zipped and empty beside the abandoned Ash Rail, its emergency heat exhausted but its seals unbroken. No blood marked the lining. No body lay in the rail cut, the ice drains, or the salt caverns below. The council declared that the storm had taken him because storms did not require a trial.

<!-- ref: ch01:p008 -->
Behind a loose vent cover, Sera found a strip of map film scratched with a route she did not recognize. Its lines crossed official streets without acknowledging them and ended beyond the western edge where civic maps printed only weather warnings. At the bottom Tev had written five numbers and the phrase WHEN THE BIRDS KEEP TIME, WALK AGAINST THEM. The paper bird on the sill continued its three-and-two rhythm. Kellan stared at the writing too long before asking what it said. Sera slid the film into her sleeve and answered, "A direction you already know."
