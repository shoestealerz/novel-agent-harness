# Chapter 3: The Ash Rail

<!-- ref: ch03:p001 -->
The abandoned platform opened behind a mosaic of destinations no living passenger remembered. Sera descended during the Registry's artificial night, when the concourse lamps cooled to blue. Advertising tiles promised orchards beneath moving suns and ocean crossings completed in a week. Mineral seep had whitened the painted faces until every traveler seemed masked. The air tasted of hot copper near the stairs and became steadily colder toward the western tunnel, as though the station still recognized the distinction between arrival and departure. Fresh boot marks crossed the ash on the rails. Between them ran narrow grooves left by a cart, and beside the grooves someone had arranged three screws, an empty space, then two more. The pattern pointed west, against the pulse recorded in the dreams.

<!-- ref: ch03:p002 -->
Sera carried the brass calibration token in her coat and Tev's film beneath her glove. At the first control box, the token's crescent nick fitted a matching tooth. The panel opened on wiring that was warm despite eighteen years without service. She did not pause to wonder how the token had left the locked Registry drawer or Kellan's neck key. The rail lights woke one by one beneath her feet.

<!-- ref: ch03:p003 -->
Beyond the panel stood a chamber ringed with map coils larger than houses. Labels identified it as the Meridian Engine, an experimental routing system built before Ilyr's public Registry. It drew power from temperature differences between the dayside and night, then translated that power into directional pulses. Tev's annotations described the citywide sleep maps as a secondary circuit. The dreams were not errors but feedback.

<!-- ref: ch03:p004 -->
The Engine could encourage thousands of sleepers to imagine the same safe path during a storm, guiding evacuations even when signs failed. It could also reinforce a route until citizens stopped perceiving alternatives. A warning diagram showed imagined corridors hardening into collective memory. Sera understood the mechanism but not its present purpose. Nothing explained who had restarted it, why the pulse followed the birds' rhythm, or whether the impossible corridor led anywhere physical.

<!-- ref: ch03:p005 -->
Kellan stepped from behind the western coil carrying a thermal lamp. He claimed he had followed her, but ash lay thick behind him and clean before him. Sera held the token like a blade. Kellan looked first at the opened panel, then at her pocket, and asked whether the drawer had been empty when she returned. It was a question shaped to expose a fact he should not have known.

<!-- ref: ch03:p006 -->
Before Sera could answer, the Engine pulsed three times. The map coils filled with amber routes, paused, then flashed twice. Every line bent west toward the cold country. In their light she saw a second set of footprints ending at a blank wall. Tev's initials had been cut into the floor there, not recently but beneath the original varnish, as though the builders had expected him decades before his birth.

<!-- ref: ch03:p007 -->
Kellan said the Engine sometimes projected probable travelers into its oldest maps. Sera asked whether probability could scratch metal. He had no answer. She pressed Tev's film against the wall and the five numbers aligned with bolts hidden beneath the plaster. The route was a set of dismantling instructions. Someone had wanted her to open the wall without teaching the Engine that a door existed.

<!-- ref: ch03:p008 -->
They removed the final bolt as the thermal storm struck Ilyr. Heat shields groaned above them, shedding flakes of old insulation that burned blue before reaching the floor. The wall folded inward, revealing a rail car with its windows painted black. Its destination board displayed no place, only the five-number sequence from Tev's film. Inside, sleeping figures appeared on the map tables—not bodies, only amber outlines repeating the gestures of boarding. One outline turned toward Sera before she moved and lifted a hand with Tev's injured thumb held rigid.
