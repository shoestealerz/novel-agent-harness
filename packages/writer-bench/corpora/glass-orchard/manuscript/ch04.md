# Chapter 4: The Unowned Window

<!-- ref: ch04:p001 -->
Orchard Master Vey summoned them before breakfast. Her office occupied a glass room suspended among the oldest branches, transparent except for one wall of smoky panes. She was small, silver-haired, and dressed without Guild gray. Sorel's brass pruning hook lay on her desk beside Pell's unsigned receipt book. Vey said Pell had delivered the hook for comparison, then disappeared. The gouge in the north gallery rail matched its edge. She offered this as evidence that Sorel fell while fighting someone who carried her own tool.

<!-- ref: ch04:p002 -->
Elian examined the hook without lifting it. Fresh gray putty filled a notch near the tip. Gallery rail paint was white. The putty instead matched the seals used on root-house fruit. Mara asked Vey why she had tested the hook before the wardens did. Vey replied that stewardship often arrived ahead of procedure. She claimed Sorel broke the rail herself while prying open a hidden conduit, lost her balance, and fell. If Pell had returned forbidden recordings, his guilt did not make Vey a murderer.

<!-- ref: ch04:p003 -->
Vey showed them the smoky wall. Each pane carried the blended murmur of hundreds of donated voices, too transformed to separate. These sheets had held through three floods. The west gate currently showed a widening harmonic fracture; without new master glass it would fail during the winter surge. Vey had selected recordings whose contracts were missing because missing contracts could not object. Sorel wanted to halt production until every donor was identified. Vey called that morality designed by someone living above the flood line.

<!-- ref: ch04:p004 -->
Mara accused Vey of silencing Sorel. Vey answered that Sorel had planned to expose the debt at the auction and destroy the Guild's authority in a single afternoon. "I tried to stop her with consequence," she said. "Someone else used height." She returned the brass hook to Mara handle first. Mara accepted it and looped the blue cord through the hole in its grip, joining truth's signal to their mother's tool. Elian noted the action because Vey watched it with something closer to recognition than fear.

<!-- ref: ch04:p005 -->
Outside, the auction had begun. Buyers raised numbered paddles while Guild readers coaxed voices from fruit: marriage promises, recipes, weather observations, one devastatingly accurate imitation of the mayor. Elian saw Dain Orro bidding through an agent on every lot harvested near the root house. When challenged, Dain said he collected evidence for the river office. His agent carried a satchel lined with lead cloth, which prevented fruit from being sounded before removal.

<!-- ref: ch04:p006 -->
Pell found them behind the furnace hall. Clay covered his boots and one sleeve was burned. He confessed that he had entered Sorel's workroom through the chimney and replaced the six recordings. He had also copied Vey's seal to remove them years earlier. But he denied touching the gallery rail or sending the threat. On the night Sorel died, he saw Dain leave the north stairs carrying a lead-lined satchel. Pell hid because Vey had ordered his arrest for theft. He still had the original ledger key sewn inside his coat.

<!-- ref: ch04:p007 -->
The key opened a drawer beneath the archive's river maps. Inside lay consent pages sorted not by donor but by acoustic strength. Many bore thumbprints; several bore only assessor marks. Their father's audit had circled forty-one disputed names. The last page carried his own signature, donating "all remaining resonance" to the west gate in exchange for safe passage beyond Bellweather. The signature looked correct. The date came three days after his official death. Mara took this as proof he survived. Elian called it proof that someone possessed his hand or his agreement.

<!-- ref: ch04:p008 -->
Alarm bells interrupted them. The west gate's fracture had begun singing below the city's hearing, a pressure felt in teeth and window frames. Vey ordered the auction fruit seized for emergency melting. Citizens closed their fists around purchases that contained births, apologies, and the last words of the dead. The Guild wardens formed a line. On the river, Dain's barge cast off despite the falling tide. Elian saw pale moths lifting from its roof in a silver cloud, and beneath them a man with a familiar stoop guiding the stern rope into the reverse current.
