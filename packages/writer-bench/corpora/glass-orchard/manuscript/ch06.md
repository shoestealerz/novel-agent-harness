# Chapter 6: The Bell Before Its Hour

<!-- ref: ch06:p001 -->
They entered the root house through the barge's trapdoor as water rose behind them. The chamber had been built around the first tree, a black trunk wider than a cottage. Copper listening pipes pierced its bark. Shelves held master fruit tagged with names, dates, and strength marks; empty spaces showed where Dain's satchels belonged. Pale moths covered the ceiling. Their wings opened whenever the gate's vibration deepened, making the room appear to breathe silver.

<!-- ref: ch06:p002 -->
Pell found Sorel's missing consent pages sealed inside a pipe. Forty disputed donors had refused the Guild's terms. The forty-first, listed only as "unowned window," was a recording taken from the public auction without any known speaker. Its resonance could repair the gate alone. Vey had ordered Pell to retrieve it, but Sorel hid it after learning the voice belonged to a child. The fruit was not in Dain's satchels. A chalk mark on the shelf matched the pale stain on the north gallery floor.

<!-- ref: ch06:p003 -->
Elian traced the chalk to a maintenance schedule. Auction bells were tested from Vey's suspended office, the furnace hall, and a hand lever on the north gallery. Sorel could have rung early while opening the conduit, but Dain said both her hands held the hook. Pell had been in the archive. Vey claimed to be inspecting the west gate. Mara had arrived at the orchard before dawn and found Sorel's body, yet she had never said where she spent the hours before it.

<!-- ref: ch06:p004 -->
Mara removed the remaining blue cord from her wrist. She had met Sorel on the gallery that night. Sorel intended to release every disputed recording through the fruit pipes, scattering evidence across the terraces before the auction. Mara feared panic would prevent repairs and begged her to wait. When Sorel refused, Mara pulled the test-bell lever to summon wardens. The vibration cracked a root beneath the gallery. The rail shifted. Mara saw Dain grab Sorel and saw cloth tear, but fled before the fall because Sorel had ordered her to protect the unowned window.

<!-- ref: ch06:p005 -->
She had hidden the fruit inside the brass pruning hook. Its handle was hollow, its white thumb wear a removable glass sleeve. Sorel gave it to Mara moments before approaching Dain. After the fall, Mara carried it until guilt made possession unbearable; then she handed it to Pell without explaining. Pell sealed its notched tip with gray putty before taking it to Vey. Vey recognized the alteration and kept the hook on her desk. Each person had held the same object while believing someone else understood its value.

<!-- ref: ch06:p006 -->
The hook remained embedded in the barge ladder outside, reachable only through water hammering the throat. Elian wanted to rescue it and use the child's voice to mend the gate. Mara refused to repeat the Guild's theft, even to save the lower streets. Their father, lucid for a moment, said a donated voice and a stolen voice vibrated identically. The difference existed in the living people who must continue beside the wall. Pell proposed sounding the fruit publicly and asking the city to choose after hearing what repair would cost.

<!-- ref: ch06:p007 -->
Vey arrived with wardens through the north passage. She had followed the barge lights and carried an emergency furnace basket. The west gate would fail within an hour. When Mara confessed to ringing the bell, Vey did not arrest her. She said accidents were luxuries assigned after survival. She ordered the master shelves cleared, including the disputed fruit. Elian stood between the wardens and the tree. He offered a different repair: tune the auction's legally purchased fruit as a temporary resonant choir, weaker than a master sheet but perhaps sufficient until donors could consent.

<!-- ref: ch06:p008 -->
The plan required hundreds of intact recordings, precise placement along the gate, and citizens willing to risk their private voices. Vey called it theater. Then the chamber shook, and a crack opened across the ceiling. Through it came the auction crowd's murmur and the high note of fruit answering the failing wall. Sound had already traveled through the old pipes to every terrace. Elian struck his lowest fork against the first tree. Across Bellweather, glass replied in uneven waves. Not harmony yet, but attention.
