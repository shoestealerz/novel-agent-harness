# Chapter 7: A Wall Made Willingly

<!-- ref: ch07:p001 -->
Pell opened the root-house speaking pipes and told the auction crowd the truth before Vey could stop him. He named the disputed donors, the false death ledger, the missing contracts, and the child recorded without consent. He also named the fracture widening through the west gate. Panic moved over the terraces, but it did not move in one direction. Some citizens carried purchased fruit toward the furnaces. Others hid theirs beneath coats. One old ferryman held up a plum containing his dead wife's laughter and asked how many minutes of wall it would buy.

<!-- ref: ch07:p002 -->
Elian calculated positions from the gate's harmonic map. Ordinary fruit varied too much to melt into one sheet, so he would leave each recording intact and seat it in the gate's empty maintenance cells. The voices had to answer one another across the fracture. Mara organized runners by terrace. Vey objected until the first stone split from the flood face and crushed an empty market stall. Then she gave Elian the Guild's private tuning table and ordered the wardens to become porters rather than guards.

<!-- ref: ch07:p003 -->
Volunteers arrived with quarrels, lullabies, debts, confessions, and sounds no ledger could price. The grandfather's snore took a low western cell. A marriage proposal repeated from the east buttress. The mayor's imitation proved useless but encouraged the crowd. Elian tuned each fruit with wax and lead thread while the river pressed the wall. No voice was melted or erased. Every donor received a numbered token promising the fruit's return if the gate survived.

<!-- ref: ch07:p004 -->
Dain emerged from the ventilation throat holding Sorel's pruning hook. The blue cord had snapped away, but the glass sleeve inside its handle remained whole. He offered it to Mara and confessed that he had suspected her role in the bell accident from the beginning. He sent the threatening message to frighten her into revealing where Sorel hid the child voice. The newer blue cord came from a roll in Sorel's workroom. His threat had imitated certainty because he possessed none.

<!-- ref: ch07:p005 -->
Mara removed the sleeve and sounded it once. A child counted orchard rows, lost place, laughed, and began again. No warning or secret justified the Guild's claim. Mara placed the sleeve in an empty fruit ring instead of the furnace basket. The gate shuddered. Elian asked for the child's recording because its strength could stabilize the entire choir without being destroyed. Mara looked toward the flooded lower streets, then toward the citizens who had volunteered what the child never could. She said no.

<!-- ref: ch07:p006 -->
Their father touched the hook's worn handle and remembered Sorel carving their initials beneath its collar. He did not remember the child voice or the bargain that exiled him. Elian understood that finding him had not restored the father preserved in family grief. The man before them was neither fraud nor answer. Mara tied a fresh piece of blue cord around his wrist, once rather than twice. It meant, from childhood, that a difficult truth could wait until morning without becoming a lie.

<!-- ref: ch07:p007 -->
The temporary choir caught the gate's failing note and divided it among hundreds of smaller resonances. Windows throughout Bellweather sang back. For a minute the city became unbearable: every private sound amplified, every secret adjacent to floodwater. Then Elian found the balancing interval, and noise settled into a chord felt more than heard. The fracture stopped widening. The wall would hold for days, perhaps weeks, long enough to build a repair from voices freely offered.

<!-- ref: ch07:p008 -->
At dawn, pale moths rested on the gate, their wings no longer mistaken for cracks. Vey resigned before the Guild could remove her. Dain surrendered the false ledgers and accepted arrest. Pell began writing receipts for the temporary choir. Families crowded the archive steps to read the disputed names, sometimes recognizing grandparents, sometimes finding only strangers whose stolen strength had sheltered them. On the terraces, volunteers guarded the borrowed fruit and compared their numbered tokens while the last flood alarms went quiet. Mara placed Sorel's hook and the unowned window in the public archive under separate locks. Elian opened his three letters in order on the lower terrace. The orchard around him repeated fragments of the night's confession, but the river below ran forward, carrying no proof that every debt had been paid. For the first time, Bellweather's wall held without pretending silence was consent.
