# Chapter 3: What the Roots Carried

<!-- ref: ch03:p001 -->
The figure in Sorel's workroom vanished before Elian and Mara crossed the bridge. The door remained locked, the window latched from within, and wet clay marked the floor below the chimney hatch. Nothing appeared stolen. Instead, six fruit now occupied the cabinet rings that had been empty that morning. Each was clouded white, as if cooled too quickly, and each bore a date from the year their father drowned. Mara barred the door. Elian listened at the chimney and heard water retreating through masonry far below.

<!-- ref: ch03:p002 -->
The first restored fruit held Sorel arguing with Orchard Master Vey. Sorel demanded repayment of "borrowed years" and threatened to open the root house during the public auction. Vey replied that Bellweather survived because some voices were allowed to become windows. The second fruit contained only a child reciting river depths. In the third, Elian's father said, "If the debt wakes, give it a name that isn't mine." The recording ended with the same three spaced knocks captured in the cracked pear.

<!-- ref: ch03:p003 -->
Restoration warmed the room and loosened older scents from the wood: resin, dust, Sorel's bitter tea. Mara kept the brass tuning fork steady while Elian worked, but her left hand trembled whenever their father's voice returned. The blue cord was back around her wrist, damp from the rain barrel. Elian asked whether she had retrieved it because she expected to lie. She said she wore it because he expected everyone else to. Neither mentioned that Pell still possessed Sorel's hook.

<!-- ref: ch03:p004 -->
The fourth fruit explained the Guild's phrase. For generations, the root house had stored master recordings donated by citizens in exchange for orchard shares. During disasters, the Guild melted those masters into resonant sheets used to reinforce levees and bridges. Voices became literal windows, walls, and flood gates. The process erased names while preserving vibration. Sorel called this theft when consent records were missing. Vey called it civic inheritance. Their father had audited the oldest donations in the month before his reported drowning.

<!-- ref: ch03:p005 -->
The fifth fruit captured Sorel alone, speaking for whoever might find it. She had not identified their father's body. The river assessor brought her a ruined face in his coat and asked whether she preferred a public corpse or a private fugitive. She chose the corpse because the Guild had accused her husband of stealing master recordings. He escaped through the ventilation throat during drought and promised to send proof. No proof came. Sorel let her children believe him dead because Vey threatened to seize the family orchard if the audit continued.

<!-- ref: ch03:p006 -->
Mara struck the table hard enough to ring every fruit. She remembered Sorel washing clay from a stranger's hands before burial and had mistaken the gesture for tenderness. Elian remembered being sent away from the cemetery because the coffin leaked. Their father's survival was now possible, but not established: the voice recordings could have been planted, the missing tooth imitated, and Sorel's confession shaped by guilt. The only independent evidence was the falsified ledger and the exposed river route. Both proved deception; neither proved who had lived beyond it.

<!-- ref: ch03:p007 -->
The sixth fruit held a recent conversation between Sorel and Pell. Pell admitted moving recordings from the root house for Vey, believing the master sheets were needed to repair the west flood gate. When he learned the selected voices belonged mostly to debtors whose consent pages were missing, he began returning fruit to Sorel in secret. He had used the chimney route that evening because Vey watched the doors. His last words were, "If I bring the hook back, she'll know you found the rail."

<!-- ref: ch03:p008 -->
Someone threw a stone through the workroom window. It missed Mara and shattered the fifth fruit against the cabinet. Its recording escaped as a confusion of Sorel's voice, river noise, and the assessor's question, then faded beyond recovery. Wrapped around the stone was a strip of blue cord, newer and darker than Mara's. A message on Orchard Guild paper read: NINE MINUTES IS LONG ENOUGH TO DROWN TWICE. Elian looked toward the root-house terrace. Pale moths gathered there despite the rain, each wing reflecting the auction furnaces like a moving shard of moon.
